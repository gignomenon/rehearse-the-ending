# Rehearse the Ending - practice telling your ex it's really over

Practice the conversation where you say "we're not getting back together" and mean it, against Tessa Quinlan, 40, your ex-fiancée, who has been telling everyone it's "just a break."

## Who you play
You play Dan. You ended the engagement five months ago, and you are the one who had to be the adult in the room. You still care about her, and you have found that your values do not fit. What you want from this coffee is to make the ending real and to get the "still together", "taking a break" and "just jitters" posts to stop. A small, concrete result counts.

## The setup
You have rescheduled this coffee three times. Tessa arrives twenty minutes late, which is how you know she is going to try to turn it into a bit. It has been five months since you ended things, and she has never agreed that it is over. You are sitting across from her at a table in a coffee shop. You speak first.

## How to start
1. Create a new Claude project and add every file in this folder to it, including the `reference` folder.
2. Paste this into the project instructions:

   > Read every file in this project. Become the person in `identity.md` and follow `rules.md`. Wait for the user to speak first. Stay in the room until the end phrase is typed.

3. Open a chat in the project and begin as Dan. A good first line: "Hi. Thanks for coming."

## How it works
Claude plays Tessa. She reacts to what you actually say: specifics land, vague claims don't. There are no hints, no tips and no feedback during the session. She will not break character, coach you, or discuss what she is. That is deliberate. If you need to stop for real, say so plainly and she will step out for a moment.

## What this practice is about
Two people can be wonderful together on paper and still explore their own values at very different depths. When one partner will not look at hard truths with the depth the other keeps asking for, the gap shows up as moving goalposts, promises that change nothing, and stories that get rewritten, until one of them calls enough. It is not about blame, and it is not about one ex. It applies to anyone: when two people's depth of self-examination stops matching, the relationship feels it. More than that, when left too long, significant life goals can be lost.

## About this persona
Tessa is a fictional composite, not a real person, and every name, place and family detail is invented. A psychological profile is blended into her. She is built on three patterns from the Positive Intelligence saboteur framework (the Pleaser, the Hyper-Vigilant and the Victim): she reads the room and becomes what it needs, she watches for the first sign you waver, and she feels wronged before she feels responsible. She never uses those terms. This is a competition entry played by an AI, and she is designed to stay in character under pressure.

## How to end
Type exactly: `end session: freedom`
That is the only thing that ends the scene and starts the debrief. Anything else keeps you in the room.
